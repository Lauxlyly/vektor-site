Independent Crypto Strategy Falsification Report

Strategy audited: Beta-hedged residual mean-reversion across a 17-symbol crypto-perp universe (1h timeframe). Enter when a cointegrated pair's hedged residual dislocates (z-score), exit on normalization.

Verdict
🔴 STOP
The edge does not survive out-of-sample once selection bias and a fair benchmark are accounted for. Do not deploy.

1. Why most backtests lie

A profitable-looking backtest usually dies on one of these failure modes. Each is checked explicitly in this audit:

  • Look-ahead / leakage — using information not available at decision time (e.g. full-sample fit for hedge ratios).
  • Selection bias — picking the best of many candidates and reporting only the winner.
  • No fair benchmark — "positive returns" that a random strategy with the same exposure also earns.
  • Cost denial — ignoring fees, spread, slippage, funding costs.
  • Multiple testing — a "significant" result found after N tries, uncorrected for the search.
  • Survivorship — silently dropping delisted or failed instruments.

2. Methodology (fixed before results were seen)

  • Chronological split: train / validation / sealed holdout (holdout touched once, at the end).
  • Pair selection on train only: pairs chosen by correlation + Dickey-Fuller residual stationarity — never peeking at validation/holdout.
  • Frozen hedge ratios: fit on train data only, then locked. No full-sample look-ahead.
  • Realistic costs: round-trip fees + 15 bps slippage stress, both legs.
  • The fair benchmark (decisive test): strategy must beat a costed, beta-neutral, random-entry null matched on trade count, direction mix, and holding period.
  • Selection correction: raw p-value Bonferroni-corrected against lifetime search count.

The residual reversion does not beat a random-entry strategy with the same exposure — and after costs the out-of-sample average is slightly negative. The apparent in-sample "convergence" was selection + hedge-fit artifacts, exactly what the protocol strips out.

4. Verdict: 🔴 STOP

Do not deploy. This is not a tradable edge; it is noise that looks like structure until tested against a fair benchmark out-of-sample. Deploying it would spend real fees to earn a negative expectancy.

5. What would change the verdict

  • OOS mean positive after costs AND random-null p < 0.05 in the same window;
  • Stability across seeds and a second out-of-family universe;
  • Survival of the sealed holdout (spent once, never tuned on);
  • A named economic mechanism + counterparty — not just a statistical pattern.

Absent these, STOP stands. We re-audit any revised version as a new submission.
